    Who it's for

    The first-time product founder.

    You're building something physical or technical. There's no research specialist on the team, no patent budget yet, and a real chance that some part of your idea is already sitting in a filing cabinet in Alexandria, Virginia. You have no practical way to find out.

    The bottleneck

    Turning a description into a shortlist.

    Patent search punishes newcomers. The records are indexed by classification codes you've never seen, described in vocabulary chosen by attorneys to be broad rather than clear, and split between granted patents and pending applications that mean very different things. So founders do one of two things: skip the search and build blind, or stall for weeks reading documents they can't interpret.

    What you get

    Evidence, not opinions.

    Publication numbers you can look up yourself. The exact sentences from each patent that touch your idea, quoted rather than paraphrased. Alternative technical routes prompted by what those patents actually claim. And a list of questions written against your specifics, ready for a professional.

    The savings

    Minutes instead of an afternoon.

    A run finishes in under three minutes. Assembling the same sourced shortlist by hand is an afternoon at best and a week at worst, and most founders never finish it. The bigger prize is the first attorney meeting: walk in with numbered records and specific questions and that hour goes to answers instead of orientation. We measure time-to-brief, source accuracy, and search cost. Legal-fee savings are the hypothesis we're testing, not a number we're claiming.

    Why it holds up

    It refuses to tell you what it can't know.

    Every comparison has to quote the patent word for word, and the quote is checked character by character against the retrieved record before it reaches you — a model can't invent a patent number or a claim here and have it survive. Coverage limits are printed on the report rather than buried. And the tool will not tell you that something is novel, patentable, non-infringing, or safe to build, because a search cannot establish any of those things. Empty results mean these keywords found nothing. Nothing more. The judgment stays with a registered patent attorney; this gets you to that conversation prepared.

    Research assistance only. Not legal advice, and not a patentability, novelty, infringement, or freedom-to-operate opinion.
